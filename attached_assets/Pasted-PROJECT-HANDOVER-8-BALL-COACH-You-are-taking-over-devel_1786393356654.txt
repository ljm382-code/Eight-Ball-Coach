PROJECT HANDOVER — 8-BALL COACH
================================

You are taking over development of an existing web application called:

8-Ball Coach

This project was originally prototyped in Claude and subsequently developed further in Bolt.new.

IMPORTANT:
This is an EXISTING PROJECT, not a request to invent a new application from scratch.

Your job is to preserve the existing concept, architecture, functionality and visual identity while completing and improving the application.

Do NOT unnecessarily redesign, simplify, remove, replace or rewrite working features.

Before changing anything:
1. Inspect every existing project file.
2. Understand the current React/Vite/TypeScript structure.
3. Identify what is already implemented.
4. Identify incomplete or broken functionality.
5. Fix existing build/type/import errors first.
6. Preserve working code wherever practical.
7. Then continue development according to the specification below.

==================================================
1. PRODUCT NAME
==================================================

8-Ball Coach

The app must NOT be branded as specifically a "Blackball Coach".

8-Ball Coach is intended to support multiple 8-ball rule systems.

The two primary rule sets currently required are:

• Blackball Rules
• International Rules

The architecture must allow additional rule sets to be added later without rebuilding the coaching engine.

Where game advice depends upon the selected rules, the coaching logic must take the active ruleset into account.

==================================================
2. CORE PRODUCT CONCEPT
==================================================

8-Ball Coach is an adaptive pool coaching and training application.

It is NOT simply:
• a drill library
• a rules reference
• a score tracker
• a generic training-plan generator

The central concept is:

THE APP LEARNS THE PLAYER'S GAME.

The player records information about:
• matches
• frames
• mistakes
• successes
• shot types
• positional problems
• tactical decisions
• recurring weaknesses
• training results

The coaching engine uses this information to build an evolving player profile.

It should determine:

"What is currently costing this player the most frames?"

Training should then be prioritised around those weaknesses.

As the player improves, the training priorities should change.

The app therefore creates a feedback loop:

PLAY
↓
RECORD
↓
ANALYSE
↓
IDENTIFY WEAKNESSES
↓
TRAIN
↓
REASSESS
↓
ADAPT TRAINING

This adaptive loop is the heart of 8-Ball Coach.

==================================================
3. PLAYER ONBOARDING
==================================================

A new user should be introduced to the app and asked which rules they normally play.

At minimum:

BLACKBALL

INTERNATIONAL RULES

The user must be able to change their preferred/default rules later.

The selected ruleset must influence relevant:
• terminology
• tactical coaching
• rules advice
• match analysis
• scenarios
• training recommendations

The welcome messaging should communicate something similar to:

"8-Ball Coach learns your game and adapts your training to fix the weaknesses that matter most."

Do not make the onboarding unnecessarily long.

==================================================
4. PLAYER PROFILE
==================================================

Maintain an evolving player profile.

The profile should be based on actual recorded performance rather than arbitrary labels.

Possible tracked dimensions include:

• potting
• cue-ball control
• positional play
• shot selection
• tactical play
• safety play
• break
• finishing
• pressure performance
• consistency
• decision making
• pace/control
• recovery after mistakes

The engine should be capable of tracking more specific weaknesses underneath these broader categories.

Examples:

• long pots
• thin cuts
• stun shots
• screw/backspin
• follow
• side spin
• speed control
• positional routes
• escaping snookers
• safety selection
• containing safeties
• attacking at the wrong time
• failed clearances
• poor first-shot selection
• poor break development
• difficult black-ball finishes

Do not treat all weaknesses equally.

The engine must consider the effect each weakness has on actual frame outcomes.

==================================================
5. ADAPTIVE COACHING ENGINE
==================================================

The coaching engine should continuously update the player's training priorities.

A weakness should become more important when factors such as these increase:

• frequency
• severity
• recent occurrence
• frame impact
• match impact
• persistence over time

The engine should distinguish between:

A player missing something once

and

A recurring weakness that is repeatedly costing frames.

Training recommendations should therefore NOT simply be based on raw mistake counts.

Recent and high-impact problems should generally matter more.

Historical data should still contribute, but older performance should gradually carry less influence than recent performance.

Do not make the system so reactive that one bad frame completely changes the player's training plan.

==================================================
6. TRAINING PRIORITIES
==================================================

The app should create ranked training priorities.

For example:

1. Cue-ball speed control
2. Long pot consistency
3. Positional route selection
4. Safety play

The user should understand WHY something has become a priority.

Where practical display explanations such as:

"Your speed-control errors contributed to 4 lost frames in your last 3 matches."

The app should feel like a coach analysing the player's game rather than an algorithm producing unexplained scores.

==================================================
7. TRAINING SESSIONS
==================================================

Training sessions should be generated from the player's current needs.

Sessions may contain several drills.

The app should avoid repeatedly serving exactly the same session.

Training should combine:

• high-priority weakness work
• reinforcement
• maintenance of existing strengths
• variation

A session should have a clear purpose.

Example:

TODAY'S FOCUS
Cue-ball speed control

WHY
This is currently your highest-impact weakness.

The player should be able to record training performance so the engine can determine whether improvement is occurring.

==================================================
8. DRILL SYSTEM
==================================================

Drills should have structured metadata rather than existing only as text.

Each drill should be able to contain information such as:

• ID
• title
• description
• category
• skill tags
• difficulty
• setup
• repetitions/attempts
• success criteria
• coaching tips
• ruleset relevance where applicable

Difficulty should be capable of adapting.

If a player repeatedly succeeds, training should become harder.

If the player repeatedly struggles, the app should avoid endlessly giving drills that are beyond the player's current ability.

==================================================
9. MATCH / FRAME LOGGING
==================================================

Logging must be quick enough that players will realistically use it.

Do NOT require excessive data entry after every frame.

The system should allow useful information to be captured with relatively few taps.

Information may include:

• win/loss
• opponent
• competition/practice
• selected ruleset
• break result
• key mistake
• shot category
• tactical error
• clearance opportunity
• clearance success/failure
• reason frame was lost
• notes

Not every field needs to be mandatory.

The purpose of logging is to provide useful coaching data.

==================================================
10. FRAME IMPACT
==================================================

A central principle is:

NOT ALL ERRORS ARE EQUALLY IMPORTANT.

For example:

Missing a difficult low-percentage shot while already losing a frame should not necessarily receive the same weighting as:

• missing a straightforward frame-winning black
• losing position during a routine clearance
• making a tactical decision that gives the opponent an easy finish

The engine should therefore support impact/severity weighting.

==================================================
11. RULE SYSTEM
==================================================

Rules must be modular.

Do not hard-code Blackball assumptions throughout the application.

Use a structure where coaching logic can reference the active ruleset.

Required initially:

BLACKBALL
INTERNATIONAL RULES

Rules-dependent tactical advice must be capable of differing between them.

Future rule sets should be addable through additional rule definitions/modules rather than major rewrites.

==================================================
12. RULES REFERENCE
==================================================

The app can include a rules reference section.

However:

The rules reference is SECONDARY to the adaptive coaching functionality.

Do not turn the app into primarily a rules encyclopedia.

Users should be able to select:

Blackball
International Rules

and view relevant guidance.

==================================================
13. DASHBOARD
==================================================

The main dashboard should immediately tell the player something useful.

Priority information includes:

• current training focus
• biggest weakness
• recent performance
• improvement
• next recommended training session

Avoid filling the dashboard with meaningless statistics.

Every prominent statistic should help answer:

"How am I playing and what should I work on?"

==================================================
14. PROGRESS
==================================================

The player should be able to see changes over time.

Useful concepts include:

• weakness trends
• skill trends
• training results
• match performance
• frame conversion
• recurring errors
• improvement areas

Where possible distinguish:

IMPROVING
STABLE
DECLINING

Do not falsely imply statistical certainty when there is very little data.

New users should receive appropriate messaging while the app is still learning their game.

==================================================
15. STRENGTHS
==================================================

The coaching engine should identify strengths as well as weaknesses.

This prevents the experience from becoming relentlessly negative.

Training should predominantly target weaknesses but should occasionally reinforce strengths.

==================================================
16. DATA CONFIDENCE
==================================================

The app should understand that five frames provide less reliable information than fifty frames.

Recommendations should therefore be capable of considering the amount of evidence available.

The UI may communicate this naturally, for example:

"8-Ball Coach is still learning your game."

Do not overwhelm the user with statistical terminology.

==================================================
17. COACHING LANGUAGE
==================================================

Use practical pool-player language.

Avoid unnecessarily technical analytics language in the UI.

Instead of:

"Your weighted error coefficient increased by 14%"

prefer:

"Speed control has been costing you more frames recently."

The underlying engine can be sophisticated.

The UI should remain understandable.

==================================================

Preserve the established visual direction from the existing project.

The current application uses a dark, premium pool aesthetic with green and gold/accent elements.

Do NOT replace this with:
• generic SaaS styling

• excessive gradients

It should feel like a serious modern coaching tool.

It should work especially well on phones and tablets.

Responsive design is required.

==================================================

22. TESTING
==================================================

The adaptive system must be tested with simulated player histories.

Test scenarios should include at minimum:

A. Brand-new player with no history.

B. Player with very little data.

C. Player repeatedly missing long pots.

D. Player repeatedly losing position through poor speed control.

E. Player making tactical errors.

F. Player whose weakness occurs frequently but rarely costs frames.

G. Player with an infrequent mistake that repeatedly costs frames.

H. Player improving rapidly in their former biggest weakness.

I. Player whose previous weakness has disappeared.

J. Player developing a new weakness.

K. Player with several similarly weighted weaknesses.

L. Player who performs well in practice but poorly in matches.

M. Player with inconsistent results.

N. Player on a long winning run.

O. Player on a losing run.

P. Player switching between Blackball and International Rules.

Q. Player recording an unusually bad single match.

R. Player returning after a long period without data.

S. Player with large amounts of historical data.

T. Player repeatedly completing a drill successfully.

U. Player repeatedly failing a drill.

V. Player with strong potting but weak positional play.

W. Player with weak potting but excellent tactical play.

X. Player with insufficient evidence for a confident recommendation.

The engine should behave sensibly in every case.

Do NOT tune the system so aggressively that one abnormal match destroys the established player profile.

==================================================
23. UX PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The player should NOT need to understand the algorithm.

They should experience:

"I play.
I tell 8-Ball Coach what happened.
It spots patterns I hadn't noticed.
It gives me the right things to practise.
It changes the training as I improve."

That is the desired experience.
