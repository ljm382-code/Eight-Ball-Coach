PHASE 4.4 — EXERCISE CONTENT & DIAGRAM INTEGRITY

Phase 4.3 is complete.

The following are APPROVED and must remain intact:

- Sage–Teal Balanced visual theme
- realistic English pool ball proportions
- English red/yellow/black/cue palette
- external training-number markers
- authored diagram support
- route overlays
- adaptive engine
- rating/confidence logic
- rules logic
- match intelligence
- Mixed Training allocation
- persistence
- navigation

This patch is NOT another redesign.

Its purpose is:

MAKE EVERY ASSESSMENT/TRAINING EXERCISE CLEAR, DISTINCT, REPRODUCIBLE ON A REAL TABLE, AND INTERNALLY CONSISTENT WITH ITS ANSWER OPTIONS.

8-Ball Coach is a TRAINING APPLICATION.

The diagrams, instructions and answer choices must therefore teach and assess real pool concepts rather than act as decorative UI.

==================================================
1. CURRENT PROBLEMS TO FIX
==================================================

Current issues observed in the live app:

A. Several decision drills reuse visually similar or identical table layouts.

B. Some diagrams do not match the question/answer options.

C. Some answers refer generically to:
- nearest ball
- farthest ball
- ball against cushion
etc.

without clearly identifying WHICH ball in the diagram is meant.

D. The current “3-Ball Yellow Clearance” screen is unclear.

It currently presents:

“Which ball?”

with Yellow 1 / Yellow 2 / Yellow 3

but does not adequately explain:

- what the player is trying to achieve
- whether this is a full clearance or only a 3-ball sequence
- whether the black is part of the exercise
- what constitutes success
- whether the player should plan the whole route
- whether the cue-ball position matters
- why one ball might be preferable to another

This is not sufficient for a real training exercise.

==================================================
2. CORE CONTENT RULE
==================================================

Every exercise must answer these questions before the player acts:

1. WHAT is the setup?
2. WHAT group am I playing?
3. WHAT is the objective?
4. WHAT exactly am I being asked to decide or execute?
5. WHAT counts as success?
6. WHAT constraints matter?

The player should be able to reproduce the exercise on a real pool table without guessing.

==================================================
3. EXTEND DRILL CONTENT METADATA
==================================================

Extend the authored drill/scenario model minimally so exercises can carry structured instructional content.

Suggested fields:

setup?: string

objective?: string

successCriteria?: string[]

constraints?: string[]

coachingFocus?: string

playerGroup?: "red" | "yellow"

assessmentInstruction?: string

trainingInstruction?: string

Do NOT put these strings directly into React conditionals.

They must live with the authored drill/scenario data.

==================================================
4. DISPLAY STRUCTURE FOR PHYSICAL DRILLS
==================================================

Before the Success / Failed interaction, physical drills should present concise instructional sections.

Recommended visual hierarchy:

DRILL NAME

SETUP
“Place Yellow 1 one diamond below the top-middle pocket. Place the cue ball directly in line, approximately two diamonds away.”

OBJECTIVE
“Pot Yellow 1 into the top-middle pocket.”

SUCCESS
“Pot the ball and keep the cue ball within the marked control area.”

Then:

[ SUCCESS ]
[ FAILED ]

Keep this concise enough for mobile use.

Do not show huge paragraphs.

==================================================
5. ASSESSMENT VS COACHING
==================================================

Assessment instructions may explain:

- setup
- objective
- rules/context
- success criteria

but must NOT reveal the correct answer.

Example:

GOOD:

“You are yellows. Identify the order that gives the largest positional margin while keeping access to the black.”

BAD:

“Start with Ball 3 because it leaves the best angle.”

The reasoning appears only AFTER the player answers.

==================================================
6. UNIQUE PATTERN-RECOGNITION SCENARIOS
==================================================

The Pattern Recognition drills must represent genuinely different table problems.

Do NOT reuse one generic 4-ball diagram with small coordinate changes.

Author distinct layouts.

At minimum:

PAT1 — OPEN-TABLE NATURAL PATTERN

Purpose:
Basic route selection.

Player:
Yellow.

Table:
3 yellows + cue + black.

Characteristics:
All three yellows are open.
One route offers clearly larger positional margins.

Example optimal sequence:
3 → 1 → 2 → black

Teaching concept:
Natural angles and minimizing cue-ball movement.

--------------------------------------------------

PAT2 — BLACK ACCESS

Purpose:
Protect position on the black.

Player:
Yellow.

Table:
3 yellows + cue + black + optional red obstacle.

Characteristics:
Two apparent routes exist.
One route leaves a poor final angle on the black.

Teaching concept:
Plan backwards from the black.

--------------------------------------------------

PAT3 — PROBLEM BALL / CUSHION

Purpose:
Recognise when a problem ball must be dealt with.

Player:
Yellow.

Table:
3 yellows + cue + black + red obstacle(s).

One yellow should genuinely be:

- close to a cushion
- partially blocked
- pocket-restricted

This must be clearly visible in the diagram.

Teaching concept:
Do not leave the problem ball until last without a route.

--------------------------------------------------

PAT4 — LOST POSITION / REPLAN

Purpose:
Adaptation.

Player:
Yellow.

Table:
A partially completed clearance with cue ball no longer in the planned area.

Teaching concept:
Do not force the original route after position has changed.

These four layouts must LOOK clearly different from each other.

==================================================
7. ANSWERS MUST MATCH THE DIAGRAM
==================================================

Every answer option must reference actual labelled balls shown in that specific scenario.

Example:

GOOD:

“Ball 3 — stun into the line of Ball 1; then use left draw from Ball 1 to Ball 2.”

GOOD:

“Ball 2 first with soft follow, then Ball 3, leaving Ball 1 as the key ball to the black.”

BAD:

“Take the nearest ball.”

BAD:

“Play the farthest ball first.”

unless the option explicitly says which labelled ball that is.

If a concept such as nearest/farthest is pedagogically useful, write:

“Ball 1 — simply take the nearest available pot and reassess.”

==================================================
8. OPTIONS AND DIAGRAM MUST BE VALIDATED
==================================================

For each decision scenario:

- every referenced Ball 1 / Ball 2 / Ball 3 exists
- every referenced shot type is plausible for its coordinates
- every claimed obstacle is actually visible
- every claimed cushion problem is actually near a cushion
- every claimed black-access issue is represented geometrically
- answer rationale matches the shown table

Do not claim:

“Ball 2 is tied to the cushion”

if Ball 2 is sitting in open space.

==================================================
9. PROBLEM-BALL IDENTIFICATION DRILL
==================================================

The current “Spot the Problem Ball” question needs a fully authored layout.

Do NOT use vague answers such as:

“The ball tucked against the cushion”

without identifying it.

Use numbered player balls.

Example:

Player:
Yellow

Yellow 1:
open near left-middle route

Yellow 2:
close to right cushion with restricted pocket access

Yellow 3:
open near lower half

Cue:
realistic starting location

Black:
relevant finishing position

Optional red:
blocks one path/pocket

Question:

“Which yellow is the key problem ball you need to plan around?”

Answers:

A. Ball 1 — because it is closest to the cue ball
B. Ball 2 — because its cushion position limits pocket and positional options
C. Ball 3 — because it is furthest from the black
D. None — all three can safely be left until later

Correct answer and rationale must correspond to the actual diagram.

11. CLEAR UP THE “3-BALL YELLOW CLEARANCE”
==================================================

The current exercise must be explicitly defined.

First decide what the exercise ACTUALLY measures.

Preferred implementation:

Rename it to:

3-Yellow Pattern Clearance

if the black is included as the finishing ball.

Setup:

“You are yellows. Place Yellow 1, Yellow 2 and Yellow 3 as shown. Place the cue ball at the marked starting position. The black remains as the finishing ball.”

Objective:

“Clear all three yellows and finish on the black.”

Task:

“Before each shot, select the yellow you intend to play.”

Success criteria:

- pot all three yellows
- finish with a playable position on the black
- minimise recovery shots
- route may change if position is lost

If the black is NOT intended to be part of this assessment, then rename the drill:

3-Ball Yellow Sequence

and explicitly state:

“The drill ends once all three yellows are potted. The black is not part of this exercise.”

Do NOT call something a clearance while leaving the player unsure whether the black must be finished.

==================================================
12. CLEARANCE INSTRUCTION SCREEN
==================================================

Before the first “Which ball?” interaction, show a short exercise brief.

Example:

3-YELLOW PATTERN CLEARANCE

YOU ARE: YELLOWS

OBJECTIVE
Pot Yellow 1, Yellow 2 and Yellow 3, then finish on the black.

HOW TO LOG IT
Before each shot:
1. Tap the ball you intend to play.
2. Play the shot.
3. Record Success or Failed.
4. If position changes, choose a new route.

FOCUS
Planning + positional execution.

[ START CLEARANCE ]

This brief should appear once at the beginning of the exercise.

Do not repeat it before every shot.

==================================================
13. NUMBER THE CLEARANCE BALLS ON THE TABLE
==================================================

The table itself must visibly show:

① beside Yellow 1
② beside Yellow 2
③ beside Yellow 3

The player should never need to infer which physical ball corresponds to the Yellow 1 button.

The selection options may then say:

Ball 1
Ball 2
Ball 3

rather than only:

Yellow 1
Yellow 2
Yellow 3

Because playerGroup is already visible.

==================================================
14. CUE BALL MUST BE SHOWN
==================================================

Any physical shot or clearance exercise must display the cue ball.

Do not present:

3 yellow balls + unrelated fourth ball

without clearly showing the cue ball.

Cue ball:

- white
- unnumbered
- positioned according to authored setup

==================================================
15. BLACK BALL MUST BE SEMANTICALLY CORRECT
==================================================

The black appears only when relevant.

If the drill includes a black finish:

show it.

If the drill does not involve the black:

omit it.

Do not use the black decoratively.

==================================================
16. CLEARANCE LAYOUTS MUST BE DISTINCT
==================================================

Different clearance difficulty levels should not just add balls in a straight line.

Author table geometry that reflects difficulty.

Example progression:

Stage 2:
two open balls with natural transition

Stage 3:
three-ball shape requiring route planning

Stage 4:
four-ball clearance with one positional transition

Higher:
one problem ball
blocked route
cluster/development
black-access constraint
recovery possibility

Difficulty must come from the table problem, not merely ball count.

==================================================
17. EXECUTION DRILL INSTRUCTIONS
==================================================

Audit all assessment execution drills.

Each must include:

SETUP
OBJECTIVE
SUCCESS CRITERIA

Examples:

STRAIGHT POT

Setup:
“Place the yellow one diamond below the top-middle pocket. Put the cue ball directly in line, approximately two diamonds away.”

Objective:
“Pot the yellow into the top-middle pocket.”

Success:
“Ball potted cleanly.”

--------------------------------------------------

STOP-BALL SPEED GATE

Setup:
“Place the target ball as shown. Mark the cue-ball target zone with two chalk markers.”

Objective:
“Pot the object ball and stop the cue ball inside the highlighted zone.”

Success:
“Object ball potted AND cue ball finishes inside the zone.”

A player must not have to infer what “success” means.

==================================================
18. POSITIONAL DRILLS REQUIRE TARGET ZONES
==================================================

A positional drill cannot be scored meaningfully if the player only knows whether the ball was potted.

Where positional success matters:

display a target zone.

Example:

Large beginner zone
Medium developing zone
Small advanced zone

Success criteria:

“Pot the object ball AND finish the cue ball in the highlighted zone.”

Use existing diagram/target-zone infrastructure or minimally extend TrainingDiagram.

==================================================
19. SPEED DRILLS REQUIRE DISTANCE/ZONES
==================================================

For speed-control exercises, define measurable targets.

Examples:

- finish within marked rectangle
- stop between two markers
- reach cushion and return to zone
- finish within one ball-width of target mark

Avoid:

“Control the speed well.”

==================================================
20. PROBLEM-BALL EXECUTION MUST SHOW THE PROBLEM
==================================================

For Simple Nudge / Cannon / Break-Out drills:

the diagram must visibly contain:

- the problem ball
- obstacle/cluster
- intended development area

Instructions must state what should happen.

Example:

“Use Yellow 1 to nudge Yellow 2 away from the cushion into the highlighted open area while retaining a shot.”

==================================================
21. BREAK DRILLS NEED REAL SETUP TEXT
==================================================

Break exercises must state:

- rack setup
- cue-ball placement
- objective
- scoring criteria

Do not rely on a tiny diagram alone to explain a break.

==================================================
22. AUTHORED SCENARIO QUALITY
==================================================

Do not mass-generate random coordinates.

Every assessment scenario should be intentionally authored.

The initial library is small.

Quality is more important than quantity.

Each scenario needs a reason to exist.

==================================================
25. OPTION QUALITY RULES
==================================================

For each 4-option decision question:

- one may be Optimal
- one may be Acceptable
- one High-risk
- one Poor

BUT the options must still be realistic choices.

Do not create silly distractors such as:

“Play by ball number”

unless a real novice might plausibly make that decision.

A poor choice should still be believable.

==================================================
26. COACHING RATIONALE QUALITY
==================================================

Every decision answer must explain:

WHY.

Include the relevant concepts:

- cue-ball travel
- natural angle
- positional margin
- black access
- risk
- problem-ball timing
- safety value
- recovery route

Avoid generic feedback like:

“This is the best pattern.”

==================================================
27. USE DIAGRAM AFTER ANSWER
==================================================

Where structured sequences exist:

after the answer, display:

- selected route
- optimal route if different
- sequence markers
- brief explanation

Example:

YOUR ROUTE
1 → 3 → 2

COACH'S ROUTE
3 → 1 → 2

Then:

“Starting with Ball 3 reduces cue-ball travel and leaves the natural stun angle into Ball 1.”

This is substantially more educational than simply saying “High risk.”
