PHASE 4.3 — TRAINING DIAGRAM & DECISION-SCENARIO REALISM

Phase 4.2 is complete.

The Sage–Teal theme, realistic English ball proportions, English red/yellow ball palette, assessment-state fix, adaptive engine, rules logic, match intelligence and persistence are APPROVED.

Do NOT redesign the application.
Do NOT change rating algorithms.
Do NOT change adaptive weighting.
Do NOT change match intelligence.
Do NOT change rules legality.
Do NOT change navigation.

This patch has one objective:

MAKE TRAINING DIAGRAMS AND PATTERN-RECOGNITION QUESTIONS ACT LIKE REAL COACHING EXERCISES RATHER THAN GENERIC GAME QUESTIONS.

==================================================
1. CORE PRINCIPLE
==================================================

8-Ball Coach is a TRAINING APPLICATION, not a game.

Every physical-table drill or decision scenario should provide enough information for a player to:

1. reproduce the position on a real table
2. identify the exact balls being discussed
3. understand the intended shot sequence
4. compare realistic tactical/pattern options
5. learn WHY one route is stronger than another

Generic diagrams with arbitrary balls and generic answers are not acceptable for authored training content.

==================================================
2. KEEP REAL ENGLISH POOL BALLS
==================================================

Do NOT convert red/yellow balls into American numbered balls.

Physical balls remain:

RED
YELLOW
BLACK
WHITE CUE BALL

Do NOT print American-style numbers directly on red/yellow balls.

Instead introduce separate TRAINING REFERENCE MARKERS.

==================================================
3. TRAINING REFERENCE NUMBERS
==================================================

For decision/pattern exercises, relevant balls may receive:

trainingLabel: "1"
trainingLabel: "2"
trainingLabel: "3"
etc.

These labels are NOT physical markings on the balls.

Render the label as a small external marker positioned next to the ball.

Example:

      ①
      🔴

or visually:

[1] beside the red/yellow ball

Use a compact circular or rounded marker:

- white/light background
- dark-teal text
- thin dark-teal border
- clearly legible
- approximately 12–16px screen size depending on table scale

The marker should sit slightly outside the physical ball.

It must NOT:

- cover the ball
- enlarge the ball
- look like an American ball number
- obscure routes or target zones

==================================================
4. BALL DATA MODEL
==================================================

Extend the display/training ball model minimally.

Example concept:

type TrainingBall = {
  id: string;
  group: "red" | "yellow" | "black" | "cue";
  x: number;
  y: number;

  trainingLabel?: string;

  role?: "target" | "obstacle" | "black" | "cue";

  playerBall?: boolean;
}

Do not alter the rules engine's fundamental ball identity.

trainingLabel is presentation/training metadata only.

==================================================
5. PLAYER GROUP MUST BE EXPLICIT
==================================================

Every pattern/clearance scenario must state which group the player owns.

Example:

playerGroup: "yellow"

Therefore:

Yellow balls = player's legal group balls
Red balls = opponent balls / obstacles
Black = final ball
White = cue ball

OR vice versa.

Do NOT present red and yellow balls as though the player can pot both groups.

If both colours appear in a diagram, the opposing colour must have a genuine tactical purpose such as:

- obstacle
- blocked pocket
- positional restriction
- cannon opportunity
- safety consideration

==================================================
6. NUMBER ONLY RELEVANT BALLS
==================================================

Training labels should primarily identify balls the question refers to.

Example scenario:

Player = Yellow

Yellow Y1:
trainingLabel: "1"

Yellow Y2:
trainingLabel: "2"

Yellow Y3:
trainingLabel: "3"

Red obstacle:
may be unnumbered unless referenced by the question.

Black:
normally labelled BLACK / visually obvious rather than included in the clearance-order numbering.

Cue ball:
never given a sequence number.

==================================================
8. DECISION OPTIONS MUST REFERENCE THE DIAGRAM
==================================================

Replace vague decision options such as:

"Take the nearest straight pot first, then reassess"

with concrete options referring to labelled balls and shot types.

Example:

A.
"Ball 3 — stun for Ball 1, then left draw from Ball 1 to Ball 2."

B.
"Ball 1 — follow through for Ball 2, then recover across table to Ball 3."

C.
"Ball 2 first with screw, then attack Ball 3 before Ball 1."

D.
"Ball 1 first, then decide the rest after the shot."

The exact wording must correspond to the actual authored diagram.

==================================================

Allow decision options to use realistic coaching terms such as:

- stun

- left side
- right side

Do not use advanced terminology where unnecessary.

Difficulty should control complexity.

Foundation scenarios:
simple terms and routes.

Advanced scenarios:
more detailed cue-ball routes and tactical considerations.

==================================================
10. OPTION DATA MODEL
==================================================

Extend authored decision options so they may contain structured route information.

Example concept:

{
  id: "routeA",

  label:
    "Ball 3 — stun for Ball 1, then left draw from Ball 1 to Ball 2.",

  tier: "optimal",

  sequence: [
    {
      ballId: "Y3",
      shot: "stun",
      positionFor: "Y1"
    },
    {
      ballId: "Y1",
      shot: "left-draw",
      positionFor: "Y2"
    },
    {
      ballId: "Y2",
      shot: "natural",
      positionFor: "black"
    }
  ],

  rationale:
    "Starting with Ball 3 gives the largest positional margin. The stun leaves a natural line to Ball 1, and the left draw from Ball 1 opens the angle to Ball 2 while preserving access to the black."
}

Do NOT require every simple scenario to use every structured field.

But authored pattern exercises should support it.

==================================================
11. RATIONALE MUST TEACH THE PLAYER
==================================================

After the player selects an answer, feedback must explain the table.

Bad:

"Correct. This is the easiest route."

Good:

"Strong choice. Starting with Ball 3 allows a simple stun into the line of Ball 1. From Ball 1, a small amount of left draw brings the cue ball back into the natural angle for Ball 2 while keeping the black accessible."

Explain:

- why the first ball matters
- cue-ball route
- risk
- positional margin
- problem-ball management
- black access when relevant

==================================================
12. SHOW SELECTED ROUTE AFTER ANSWER
==================================================

Where route sequence data exists, after the player answers:

highlight the chosen/optimal sequence on the table.

Example:

① Ball 3
↓
② Ball 1
↓
③ Ball 2
↓
BLACK

Use subtle route lines/arrows.

Do not animate excessively.

This is instructional feedback.

==================================================
13. ROUTE LINE VISUALS
==================================================

PoolTable should support optional:

routeSegments

Example:

{
  fromBallId: "Y3",
  toBallId: "Y1",
  type: "cueBallRoute"
}

Use:

- thin teal/dark-teal line
- arrowhead
- optional dashed style for cue-ball travel
- low opacity so balls remain visible

Do not imply physically exact ball trajectories unless the scenario explicitly defines them.

The route is a coaching diagram, not physics simulation.

15. PATTERN RECOGNITION ASSESSMENT
==================================================

Update Pattern Recognition assessment items so the player is genuinely tested on table reading.

==================================================
16. DO NOT CONFUSE TRAINING LABEL WITH POT ORDER
==================================================

Important:

Ball numbers identify balls.

They do NOT inherently prescribe the correct sequence.

Ball 1 is simply reference Ball 1.

The correct route could be:

3 → 1 → 2

or:

2 → 3 → 1

This is essential because otherwise players will assume numbering itself indicates the answer.

Add small explanatory text on first use:

"Numbers identify the balls — they do not show the correct order."

This may appear once during assessment/training and should not interrupt every scenario.
